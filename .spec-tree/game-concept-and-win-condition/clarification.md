# Clarification — Game Concept and Win Condition

## Clarified Decisions

| Question | Clarification |
|---|---|
| Is the game area control? | Not necessarily. The concept allows objectives, routes, tokens, influence, or hybrid systems. |
| Is “Claim” just points? | No. It is a player-facing victory representation that must be visualized through game objects and explained by completed objectives. |
| Can the match end early? | No. Root constraints require the match to remain open until late/final phases. |
| Does the branch choose exact mechanics? | No. It chooses the win-condition direction and quality constraints; child nodes refine mechanics. |
| Can comeback be artificial rubber-banding? | No. Comeback must come from strategic timing, changing public objectives, contested opportunities, or exposed leader vulnerabilities. |
| Can UI show numbers? | Yes, but numbers cannot be the only representation. The game must show banners/seals/tokens/progress states visually. |

## Open Questions for Child Nodes

1. How many public objectives are visible at once?
2. How does the Claim display show lead, danger, and comeback path?
3. What objective rotation cadence prevents one dominant plan?
4. What visible theme objects best communicate Arabic-first strategic identity?
5. How are team Claim and individual Claim represented in 2v2?

## Terms

- **Claim**: visible victory progress earned through strategic objectives.
- **Majlis**: final ceremonial reveal/metaphor for match conclusion; not necessarily a literal UI screen.
- **Public Objective**: visible strategic opportunity that changes context and guides player choices.
- **Comeback Window**: late-game opportunity that rewards preparation and risk, not random compensation.
